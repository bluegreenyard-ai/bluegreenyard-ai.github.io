From Forecast to Automatic Water Management

Weather forecasts, sensor data and hydraulic control are combined to manage storage capacity before and during rain events.

Development

The rainwater storage system has been extended from manual operation to an automated management system. Forecast data, current tank levels and the hydraulic network are combined to decide whether water should remain stored, be transferred or be discharged.

🧮 Calculation

If required, pumps and valves automatically create additional storage capacity.

Forecast-Based Storage Management

Each tank receives a different amount of rainwater because different roof areas are connected to the storage system. For this reason, a tank-specific inflow factor is used.

Required storage release

max( 0, current volume + expected rainfall inflow - usable storage capacity )
Demand-based control:
Water is not automatically discharged simply because rain is forecast. Only the amount that is expected to exceed the available storage capacity has to be moved.

Self-Learning Rainfall & Inflow Model

The calculation does not remain static. After real rain events, forecast data, measured rainfall and the actual tank response can be compared. The system therefore continuously learns how much water actually reaches each tank during rainfall.

1. Forecast Validation

The predicted rainfall is compared with the rainfall that actually occurred.


This makes it possible to evaluate how closely the weather forecast represented the real rain event.

Continuous adaptation:
The system does not simply replace the existing factor after one rain event. After several valid events, the recent measurements are evaluated using a robust median and the resulting value is adopted gradually. This reduces the influence of individual sensor errors or unusual rain events.
The learning loop
Forecast → Prediction → Real Rain → Tank Response → Learning → Improved Inflow Factor → Next Forecast

Automatic Storage Control

In automatic mode, the system regularly evaluates the forecast and the current tank levels. If storage capacity is insufficient, an appropriate transfer route is selected and executed automatically.

Forecast-Based Transfer

Expected rain is converted into expected tank inflow. Only the required storage volume is created.

Automatic CWH Supply

The CWH reservoir is automatically refilled when its defined start level is reached.

Overflow Protection

A measured tank level of 95 % or more triggers an additional reactive protection mechanism.

Sensor-Based Stop

During pumping, current tank levels are monitored. The calculated runtime serves as an additional fallback.

Next Step: Rainwater Inventory

The current system primarily manages water quantity: How much water is available, how much storage capacity is free and how much rain is expected?


The next development step is to extend this quantitative management with information about water age and water quality.

From Quantitative to Quality-Oriented Management

When storage capacity is limited, the primary objective is quantitative management: sufficient volume must be available for the next rainfall event.


However, when sufficient rainwater is available, the management strategy can increasingly consider water quality. Instead of only asking "Where is free capacity?", the system can also ask "Which water should be retained?".

Future decision logic
Quantity + Water Age + Water Quality + Rain Forecast → Decision

Keep

Water with sufficient quality can remain stored.

Transfer

Water can be moved to another tank depending on storage demand and quality.

Use

Suitable water can be prioritised for irrigation, cooling or other applications.

Replace

Older or lower-quality water could be used or discharged before fresh rainwater enters the system.

Vision: Intelligent Rainwater Inventory

The long-term goal is a digital inventory that describes not only how much rainwater is stored, but also its age, quality and expected future inflow.

How much water? → How old? → What quality? → How much rain is coming? → What should the system do?
Development direction:
From a purely quantitative storage management system towards an adaptive and quality-oriented rainwater management system that can decide which water should be stored, transferred, used or replaced.
